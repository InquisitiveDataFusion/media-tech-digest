United States & Canada
Deals & business

Paramount has finalised its $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, creating a combined media giant named Skydance. The transaction brings together major film studios alongside prominent television networks such as CBS, CNN, MTV and the Food Network. RedBird invested an additional $4 billion to support the takeover, bringing its total commitment to $6 billion.

As part of the post-merger strategy, Skydance plans to unify HBO Max and Paramount Plus into a single streaming service over time. However, building the necessary technology stack is expected to take a while. The newly appointed heads of the motion picture group have pledged that both Paramount Pictures and Warner Bros. will continue to build and release their own films.

The deal has received positive feedback from President Donald Trump, who praised the transaction and the leadership team. Meanwhile, a longshot bid by subscribers to block the merger has been filed with the Supreme Court. The combined company faces the challenge of managing an $80 billion debt load while attempting to achieve $6 billion in cost savings.

Results & money

Fitch Ratings downgraded the credit ratings of both Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery immediately after the transaction closed. The ratings agency pointed to materially higher leverage and significant execution risks. It also highlighted uncertainty surrounding the company's ability to successfully achieve its cost-saving targets.

Skydance leadership has already warned staff that layoffs are on the horizon. A joint memo from chief executive David Ellison and co-chief executive Ynon Kreiz confirmed that job cuts will be made to streamline the combined business.

Regulation & policy

The legal battle between Disney and the Federal Communications Commission has moved to the federal courts. Disney is attempting to block the agency from reviewing its broadcast licences. The company's top lawyer, Horacio Gutiérrez, is leading the First Amendment clash against the administration.

In Canada, Telus and AST SpaceMobile have completed their first successful satellite-to-smartphone integration test. Meanwhile, Disney+ Canada has launched an out-of-home advertising campaign to make its Hulu content more visible to local subscribers.

Technology & AI

Google has partnered with Constellation in a 20-year agreement to update six nuclear power plant sites across the United States. The deal aims to secure clean electricity for Google's power-hungry data centres. In the consumer space, Google is launching its Xreal Aura transparent XR glasses to compete directly with Meta's wearable devices.

Microsoft has unveiled its Surface Laptop Ultra, developed in partnership with Nvidia. The premium laptops run on Nvidia's RTX Spark chips and are designed to run advanced artificial intelligence models. The entry-level model starts at $2,599.

Google has also acknowledged that its new Intel-based Googlebook laptops may experience issues running certain Android apps. This admission came on the day the devices hit the market, despite previous marketing emphasizing full access to the Play Store.
