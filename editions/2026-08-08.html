United Kingdom
Deals & business

The UK Competition and Markets Authority has cleared Paramount Skydance's proposed acquisition of Warner Bros Discovery [15]. Culture secretary Lisa Nandy confirmed she will not intervene in the eighty-five billion pound deal [16].

The decision follows guarantees secured by the government regarding the future of Channel 5 [16].

Results & money

ITV has reported solid first-half financial results in its first update since announcing its deal with Sky [3]. The broadcaster maintained its full-year guidance, aided by an advertising boost from the FIFA Men's World Cup [3].

However, new regulations on less healthy foods reduced its revenue by twenty million pounds [3].

Regulation & policy

A coalition of eighteen media businesses has written to the Culture Secretary to oppose plans for BBC podcast advertising [11, 35]. The group, which includes broadcasters, audio producers, and news publishers, warned the proposals pose serious risks to the wider audio industry [2].

Audience & ratings

The BBC has partnered with ATHLOS to become the official UK broadcast partner for the league's first international event in London [5]. The public broadcaster will stream the all-women's professional track and field event live from StoneX Stadium [5].

To combat declining youth engagement, the BBC has hired twenty-six-year-old entrepreneur Brandon Relph for its strategy team [4]. Relph will help the broadcaster compete with content creators on platforms like YouTube and TikTok [4].

United States & Canada
Regulation & policy

The Federal Communications Commission voted two to one to repeal the rule limiting broadcasters from owning stations reaching over 39 percent of US households [17]. The agency will now assess future deals individually using a public interest standard rather than a fixed percentage cap [18]. Critics worry this shift could negatively impact smaller, local television markets [18].

Meanwhile, a federal judge has ruled that Nexstar executives are barred from joining the board of Tegna [13]. The ruling maintains an existing order to keep the two broadcasting companies separate [13]. Separately, the UK government has approved Paramount's proposed takeover of Warner Bros.
