Regulation & policy

The BBC and ITV have joined a report calling on the UK government to invest in digital infrastructure. The broadcasters argue that a managed digital transition will improve connectivity and participation.

At the same time, independent YouTube creators are fighting proposals that would favour traditional broadcasters in video platform algorithms. Creators argue the changes would unfairly disadvantage online-first content.

United States & Canada
Deals & business

The proposed merger between Paramount and Warner Bros Discovery is facing intense political and legal pressure. Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass has urged California Attorney General Rob Bonta to resolve the multi-state antitrust lawsuit blocking the deal [9, 66]. However, Bonta maintains that any settlement outside of court will require robust structural remedies [11].

Local economic assessments have raised further concerns about the transaction. A report from Los Angeles County projects that the merger could threaten 2.78 billion dollars in economic value, including 1.26 billion dollars in lost wages [25]. Despite these warnings, Paramount is preparing to enter early settlement talks with California officials [26].

Regulation & policy

The Federal Communications Commission is currently scrutinising a programming move by Disney-owned ABC in St. Louis [27]. In response, ABC has filed a First Amendment lawsuit against the regulator [17]. A federal judge has planned a hearing for early October, which could occur sooner if the commission acts on ABC's station licences [17].

In Canada, the CRTC has blocked an attempt by Videotron to destandardise four of its lower-speed internet tiers [14]. The regulator denied the Quebecor-owned company's request to phase out the aggregated third-party access speeds [14]. Meanwhile, FCC Commissioner Gomez warned that satellite deployments cannot replace universal service obligations [28].

Technology & AI

Walmart has reversed its long-standing opposition to mobile wallets by agreeing to support Apple Pay and Google Pay [13]. The retail giant will launch tap-to-pay services at select locations on 24 August, with a full rollout planned by the end of 2026 [12].

Artificial intelligence infrastructure is expanding rapidly across North America. AI developer Anthropic is hiring staff to establish data centres in Alberta, Canada, aiming to bring gigawatts of compute to the region [16]. Additionally, chipmaker Nvidia has partnered with data centre developer Cloverleaf to support ongoing AI infrastructure demands [29].

Radio & audio

Canadian media group Corus Entertainment has confirmed another round of layoffs across its television and radio divisions [15]. These cuts follow dozens of redundancies in July as the company restructures its Global News and talk radio operations [33, 37].

In the United States, Cumulus Media has received FCC approval to transfer its broadcast licences to new shareholders, clearing its path to exit bankruptcy [30]. Concurrently, a federal appeals court denied a rehearing bid by Nielsen in its ongoing antitrust dispute with Cumulus [31].
