United Kingdom
Deals & business

ITV has struck a major content deal with Warner Bros Discovery to bring more than 2,000 hours of TLC and Quest programming to ITVX. The content will be available to UK viewers over the next 12 months through dedicated branded collections on the streaming platform.

ITV has also acquired the AMC original crime drama Parish for its streaming service. The six-part series, starring Giancarlo Esposito, is scheduled to premiere on ITVX on 1 November.

Regulation & policy

The Digital, Culture, Media and Sport Committee has warned that the BBC risks a future of managed decline without a fundamental funding overhaul. MPs argued that the licence fee should no longer be linked to owning a television set, suggesting the government look at a household levy instead.

Meanwhile, West Ham co-owner David Sullivan has launched a 150 million pound High Court lawsuit against the BBC. The legal action follows a Panorama documentary that featured several women accusing him of abusing his power.

Audience & ratings

Morale at the BBC has been severely damaged by a gradual redundancy process that will see up to 2,000 jobs cut. Staff are increasingly concerned that the corporation will announce the closure of BBC Three and BBC Scotland next week to help meet a 500 million pound savings target.

The potential closures would see BBC Three return to an online-only format. The channel, which is the home of hit shows like Gavin and Stacey and Fleabag, has suffered from poor broadcast ratings since its return to linear television.

Technology & AI

Vodafone has officially launched its new television service in the UK. The service is delivered via a 4K Android TV set-top box and integrates the new free-to-air streaming platform Freely alongside subscription apps like Netflix and HBO Max.

Separately, Comcast has made cuts to its streaming technology teams ahead of its planned acquisition of ITV. While most of the job losses are at Sky, some US-based NBCUniversal employees are also affected.

Radio & audio

Derren Brown is set to present a new ten-part series for BBC Radio 4 and BBC Sounds. The series will explore the life, publicity, and reinvention of the famous illusionist Harry Houdini.

In the independent sector, Angel Radio is expanding its DAB footprint to 21 new locations. The expansion was made possible after two listeners left money to the station in their wills.

United States & Canada
Deals & business

Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery will rename their combined business Skydance when their 110 billion dollar merger closes. Wall Street banks have completed a 52 billion dollar debt sale to support the transaction. Former Mattel chief Ynon Kreiz will serve as co-CEO with a 5 million dollar base salary.

The new leadership structure places Warner Bros. TV, CBS Studios, and Paramount Television Studios under George Cheeks. Channing Dungey will take over the Paramount cable networks, while Casey Bloys is expected to manage the streaming portfolio. However, film chiefs Michael De Luca and Pamela Abdy will depart the company.

Audience & ratings

Disney and Netflix have agreed a major licensing deal to bring library titles and current-season television shows to Netflix. The agreement includes series such as Percy Jackson, Will Trent, and Shifting Gears. In a separate executive move, Disney communications executive Naomi Bulochnikov-Paul has joined Netflix.

Peacock has greenlit a scripted television series based on the Fast and Furious franchise, targeting a 2028 premiere. The streaming service is also bringing back its free trial offer, though it will not be available directly on the platform. Additionally, Nexstar and DAZN will broadcast 15 Indiana Pacers games.

Technology & AI

OpenAI has introduced Dots, a new artificial intelligence agent powered by its GPT-6 Astra model. The launch directly targets Meta, whose Muse platform has enjoyed early success. Publishers are also pushing for transparency, introducing the SPUR standard to track how AI models use their content.

Amazon Web Services chief Matt Garman has defended the company's data center expansion against local environmental backlash. Amazon has ended its use of non-disclosure agreements but warned that blocking data centers could harm national security. Meanwhile, the Federal Communications Commission has eased environmental reviews to speed up wireless rollouts.
