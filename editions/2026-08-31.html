Deals & business

Sky has received planning permission for its new Livingston Campus in Scotland. The development will bring together existing operations into a modern, sustainable workplace for around 3,000 employees.

In sports rights, the England and Wales Cricket Board is pushing to return more live Test cricket to free-to-air television. ITV is reportedly open to broadcasting matches in the next rights cycle starting in 2029.

Regulation & policy

BBC Studios and Sky have strongly condemned unlawful streaming after a UK man was jailed for selling illegal IPTV services. Milan Ibrahim was sentenced after making nearly one million pounds from the illicit operation.

Meanwhile, the Children's Media Foundation has warned that financial penalties are not enough to protect young people online. The warning follows a major US court settlement where Meta agreed to pay penalties and reform its platforms to reduce teen harms.

Technology & AI

New BBC director general Matt Brittin has proposed a radical shift to open up the iPlayer platform to user-generated content. This content would be curated by a public service algorithm designed to remain free from the commercial interests of US technology giants.

To support this digital push, the BBC is recruiting its first head of multiplatform commissioning. The broadcaster is also seeking YouTube-first funny entertainment formats from independent producers to replicate the success of popular digital hits.

BBC Radio 6 Music will also premiere a revised recording of Pet Shop Boys' Turing tribute, A man from the future, this October. The piece explores the life and influence of the pioneering mathematician Alan Turing.
