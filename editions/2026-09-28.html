United Kingdom
Deals & business

Netflix has ramped up its UK strategy, hosting its first Upfront event in London to pitch to advertisers. The streamer also unveiled a new slate of local commissions, including projects with Charlie Brooker and Sarah Lancashire.

Channel 5 is reshaping its portfolio by launching 5Drama to replace 5Select. Elsewhere, Sky Sports has secured a renewal for exclusive UK and Ireland coverage of international cricket from South Africa for the 2026/27 season.

Regulation & policy

The BBC faces a potential funding overhaul, with reports suggesting the licence fee could be replaced by a broadband levy. Meanwhile, the corporation is preparing to cut various TV and radio services to achieve £500 million in savings, with BBC Three again at risk.

Ofcom has published findings showing that consumers and small businesses have mixed feelings about the use of AI in customer services. Additionally, TikTok has officially dropped its appeal against a £12.7 million fine issued by the UK data watchdog for failing to protect underage users.

Radio & audio

BBC Local Radio is set to share its late show with BBC Radio 5 Live. This move is part of the broader operational changes occurring within the corporation.

Boom Radio is closing its Boom Rock station at the end of September. The parent company is instead investing £1 million into a listener-led campaign for its main station.

United States & Canada
Deals & business

Paramount Skydance is preparing to close its $111 billion merger with Warner Bros. Discovery despite ongoing legal challenges. The company has set a target date of 5 October to issue shareholder warrants tied to the deal. However, the board noted that the exact timing for the close is not yet certain.

A coalition of advocacy groups has filed legal requests to block the merger. They submitted an amicus brief asking the court to reject the settlement Paramount reached with twelve state attorneys general. Internal morale at Warner Bros. remains low as staff wait for the brand to survive the transition.

Regulation & policy

A federal judge issued a temporary restraining order against the White House to restore press credentials for several news outlets. Journalists from CNN, MS NOW and Politico were initially excluded from a state dinner for the Chinese President. The court ruled that the ban must be lifted immediately.

Broadcasters face a deadline for FCC regulatory fees with no further relief for AM and FM stations. Payments must be submitted through the CORES system by midnight to avoid a 25 percent penalty. The FCC is also preparing to vote on new rules for satellite spectrum and ultra-wideband devices.

Audience & ratings

The Traitors: New Blood delivered the strongest unscripted premiere for NBC in six years. The civilian version of the show reached over five million new viewers across Peacock and linear platforms. In the streaming charts, Reacher maintained its lead by topping one billion viewing minutes for five consecutive weeks.

Middle school students have started using the comment sections of NPR podcasts on Spotify as makeshift group chats. Staff discovered the trend after noticing unusual activity that they initially mistook for automated bots. The phenomenon highlights how younger users find workarounds when blocked from traditional social media apps.

Technology & AI

Meta is launching new VR glasses that provide a cinema experience with access to services like Prime Video and Peacock. The device will offer virtual and 3D enhancements for sports content from the NBA and MLB. This move aims to consolidate various streaming platforms into a single wearable device.

Google is expanding its infrastructure into orbit with the Suncatcher orbital data centre test. The experimental satellite will use Tensor Processing Units to handle AI queries for short bursts in space. This project evaluates if sensitive computing equipment can survive the rigours of a rocket launch.
